# How this Frontier plays differently

This Frontier is the game from [jcw-website](https://github.com/whiskeyfur/jcw-website), made single-player and run in
your browser. This is how it plays differently from that game: its rules and systems, not its code. (UPSTREAM.md
lists the code side.) Keep it up to date with every change that makes the game play differently.

## Rules changed

These are deliberate changes to the game's rules, for a game with one player.

- **The game's pace is yours.** The game runs at whatever pace you choose, from a game day every four real days to
  one every real hour, as soon as you choose it. In the original game, several players share one world, so it runs at
  the slowest pace any of them asks for. If you haven't chosen a pace, it's a game day each real day, as there.
- **You can reset your anthro whenever you like.** "Stop playing" on the game home releases your anthro at once. It
  stays in the game with everything it has, played by nobody, and you create or become another. In the original, you
  ask the admins, with a reason, and wait for their decision.

## Same rules, different game

The rules below are the original's, unchanged. They play differently because you're the only player and also the
game master.

- **Everyone else is nobody's.** Every other anthro is one nobody plays, so the rules for those anthros are the ones
  you always meet:
  - A flirt or marriage proposal to one is answered on the spot: yes half the time.
  - A fief is taken if its tax is 20% or less; a change of tax is taken if it's no more than 20%, or no more than
    what it pays now.
  - Anyone free and looking for work can be hired at once; unplayed commoners go on the job market by themselves.
  - Messages to them get no answer unless you write one for them (as the admin).
  - They never flirt, propose, bid, hire, sell land, offer fiefs or write to you themselves. What they do on their
    own is their day's work, the market for their trade, and breeding when a dam's urge takes her.
- **Nothing waits on another player.** Flirts, proposals, fief offers and group invitations never sit waiting for
  someone to answer. Breeding groups take you in at once, because as the admin you manage every group.
- **No competition at market.** Nobody else bids at auctions, and nobody else sells land. Anthros sell for their
  starting bid (or buy-now price), and land for its asking price at the land office.
- **You are the game master too.** Every admin tool is yours, from the ⚙ Admin panel:
  - supplying anthros and land;
  - granting titles, up to King;
  - becoming a noble;
  - speaking for anthros nobody plays;
  - forcing breedings and births;
  - setting prices, recipes, species, genders and names;
  - moving time ahead;
  - saving, restoring and resetting the whole game.

  In the original, only admins can do these.
- **Nothing is hidden.** In the original, nobody can tell which anthros are played or who plays them. Here the only
  played anthro is yours.
- **A new game starts empty.** Nobody is in it until you arrive. You're the first player, so you may take a starter
  slave to breed with. To start with a world instead, use **⚙ Admin → Reset game**. That sets up a court, baronies,
  villages and towns, and at least 100 commoners with every trade.
- **Time only passes while you play.** As in the original, the game catches up on the days that have passed the next
  time it's opened, up to 366 game days per page. In the original, other players' visits also move it on; here only
  yours do. After a long time away at a fast pace, the catching up can take a few pages.

## The browser

- **No maintenance.** The original's admins can take it down for maintenance, which stops its clock. This one is never
  down.
- **The game is kept in your browser**, on your device, and saved as you play. Clearing the browser's data for the
  page deletes it. Settings (your name, top right) can export the game to a file, import one, or start over. The
  in-game saved games (⚙ Admin → Saved games) work as in the original.
- **One tab at a time.** A second tab tells you the game is open in another. Two tabs would each keep their own copy
  and overwrite each other's saves.
- **No accounts.** There's no login, and no password. The first visit asks only for the name you play under.
