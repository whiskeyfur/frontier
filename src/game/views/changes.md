## 2026-09-26

### Resetting your anthro
- **Stop playing your anthro whenever you like,** from the game home: no request to wait on. It stays in the game with
  everything it has, and you create or become another.

### Your pace
- **The game's pace is yours to set:** it runs at the pace you choose (under Preferences, or on the clock), at once,
  with nobody else to wait for. If you haven't chosen one, a game day each real day.

### A peopled world
- **A new game starts with at least 100 commoners,** free and nobody's, each a Journeyman at a trade: first enough
  farmers (given 10 acres each), fishers, hunters and swineherds to feed everyone, then **every other trade**.
- **A new anthro can choose a skill**, and starts a Journeyman at it: it can work its trades at once.
- **Choosing an anthro to become** shows each one's job and level.
[[only: admin]]
- **Reset game** sets at least how many commoners, villages, towns and cities the new game has (beside the court):
  the court's own count, and the rest are added, spread over the baronies.
[[/only]]

### The game's own calendar
- **The game's time runs apart from ours,** on its own calendar: this game moved to **1 January 1200** (everyone's age,
  and every date, moved with it). A reset starts a new game on a date the admins choose.
- **Choose the game's pace** under **Game → Preferences**: game days for each real day, from 0.25 to 24. The game runs
  at the slowest pace anyone playing asks for (a game day a real day if nobody asks).
- **Every game day happens,** however many pass between visits: meals, work, taxes and the rest, each in turn. (Before,
  days nobody visited were skipped.)
- **Auctions keep real time,** so there's always time to bid.
- **A running clock** at the right of each section's bar shows the game's date and time, with buttons to ask for a
  pace under it: the pace in effect pulses, yours is solid. The top of this page also says when the next game day
  begins.
[[only: admin]]
- **Advance time** now moves the game's calendar on (dates in the game stay as they are), and **Reset game** asks for
  the new game's first day.
[[/only]]

### Work makes goods
- **Occupations are producers, craftsmen or service jobs.** Producers make goods from their labour: a farmer grows
  grain, hops, flax or vegetables (on at least 10 acres of its master's land), a miner digs iron ore, stone, clay or
  gold, and so on. Craftsmen turn goods into goods: a miller makes flour from grain, a brewer ale from grain and hops.
  Service jobs (a scribe, a priest, a soldier...) need no materials, make nothing, and are paid in coins as before.
- **The goods are the pay:** producers and craftsmen earn no coins, but keep what they make, or sell it at the
  market. A day makes 1, 2, 3 or 5 units by level. Materials come from the master's store; short of them, it makes
  what they allow, or rests.
- **Choose the recipe** when planning work ("Farmer: Hops"); otherwise it works the first.
- **36 new goods** at the market, from grain to jewellery. **Vegetables, meat, fish, bread and hot meals feed
  anthros** like food does: each eats one a day, the cheapest first.
- Anthros that keep themselves buy their materials, sell what they make, and never take up a trade that needs land.
- Taxes can be paid in any goods, after coins and spare food.
- **Market → Goods** sorts by any column (click its heading) and filters (the box under each heading: type "food"
  under Food for just the food goods).
[[only: admin]]
- **Admin → Recipes:** what each occupation makes, from what; and **Admin → Goods** marks which goods are food.
[[/only]]

### Deleting notifications
- Deleting notifications now says what it does: they're gone from your list, but administrators can still review them.

### Workers are hired to work
- **Employers can no longer breed their employees,** or plan them to breed; the job market's "may be bred" terms are
  gone, and so is the coin more that came with them. Wages are **1 to 15 coins a day** (a Master's pay). A free
  employee's cubs are her own.

### Fixes
- **An anthro that dies while up for auction** ends its auction, and the highest bid now goes back to the bidder (it
  was lost).
- **An anthro's goods** (food, lumber) now go to its heir when it dies, with its coins.
- **Clearing land** adds to its master's own land there, never to a fief held of a lord.
- **An anthro's own pages** (its page, schedule and breeding), an auction, a barony and the game home now show their
  section's bar under the menu, marking the page they belong to.

### Docs
- **Changes** has moved under a new **Docs** menu, beside a new **Knowledge Base**: how each of the game's systems
  works, from time and schedules to land, titles and taxes.

### Page layout
- A section's pages (Assets, Market, Court...) are now a **bar under the menu**, instead of tabs on the page. On a wide
  screen, the menu and that bar stay at the top as you scroll; on a phone the bar is hidden, and the menu (☰) lists
  the same pages.

### Maintenance
- The game can be taken down for maintenance (updates are one such time). Every page then says so instead, and
  nothing happens in the game until it's back: no days go by, so no one misses a meal or a day's work.

### Looking for work
- On the game home, **choose the work you offer** when looking for work: any occupation whose skill you've learned,
  with your level at it. The job market shows it, and an employer who hires you starts you on it every day (until
  they plan otherwise).
- **Wages follow the work:** each skill you can offer shows what you can expect to ask for it at your level (what
  that work pays: Novice 2, Apprentice 4, Journeyman 8, Master 15 coins a day), and a coin more if you may be bred.
  Wages go up to 16 coins a day. Workers nobody plays ask by the same rule, from their best skill. The form also says
  what food costs at the market.

### The daily tick
- The top of this page says when the game's day turns over: **00:00 UTC**, with the first visit after it, and how long
  until the next.

## 2026-09-25

### Who manages what
- **Knights** manage villages, expanses and the new **manors** (a smaller village around a knight's estate);
  **baronets and higher** manage towns and cities. A manager below the rank its part needs lets it go.
- A reset that seats a court gives each baronet a town or city, and each knight a village, manor or expanse, in the
  barony of the baron they serve.

### Finances
- **Wallet** is now **Finances**, with the Wallet and a new **Report**: what your anthro can expect to earn and spend over
  the next week, from what's planned now: your household's work, wages paid and earned, food and meals to buy, and
  taxes to your lord and from your vassals, with your balance before and after.
- **Donate:** an anthro's page has a **Donate** button, to give it coins or goods. Coins go to its own wallet; goods to
  the store it's fed from (its owner's, if it's owned).

### Growing up
- **Default days by age**, for anthros nobody has planned: under **5 weeks**, a newborn only rests (its days can't be
  planned). Then it **learns a trade from a parent**: a boy from his father, a girl from her mother (the other parent,
  if that one can't teach; a trade of its own, if neither can), until it's a Journeyman at it (28 days). Then it
  **works that trade**. These show on its schedule, until someone gives it a routine.

### Anthros that keep themselves
- An anthro that owns itself and isn't played (nor employed, nor on a schedule of its own) **lives by a trade**: a
  random occupation it works at as at least a **Journeyman**. Its routine is working it every day (it shows on its
  schedule, until someone gives it another). With no food and no coins for a meal, it forages instead. It **buys
  food** at the market with what it earns, toward a week's worth.

### The goods market
- **Market → Goods:** buy goods from the market, and sell them to it, at its prices. They go into, and come out of, your
  anthro's store.
- A good never sells for more than it costs.
[[only: admin]]
- Admins manage the goods and their prices (**Admin → Goods**): what the market charges for each and what it pays, or
  neither. Admins can add new goods.
[[/only]]
- **Meals** bought when the food runs out cost food's market price, and a vassal's **taxes** value food and lumber at
  what the market pays for them (to start: food and lumber cost 5 coins and sell for 2).

[[only: admin]]
### Admins speak for anthros nobody plays
- On the admin Notifications page, admins can **send a message as an anthro nobody plays**, and messages to one have
  a **Reply as** button. Players see it from the anthro, like any message; only admins see who wrote it.
[[/only]]

### Fixes
- **Schedules are carried out once a day for the whole game,** with the rest of the day's business. A routine set up
  (or a day planned) after that starts tomorrow; before, it was carried out at once, so a new anthro could forage or
  work on the day it arrived.
[[only: admin]]
- **Advancing time** now moves the day of the week on too: each day advanced is the next weekday in the game. Before,
  every advanced day ran as the real day of the week, so a routine's plan for that one weekday (a Saturday of
  breeding, say) was carried out every day advanced, as were dams' conceiving days and the Monday tax day.
[[/only]]
- A weekly routine (an anthro's, or a standard schedule's) that can't be saved keeps everything entered, with the day
  in error marked, instead of going back to the saved routine. Planning a day keeps its choice too.
- An anthro's page offers **Schedule** only to whoever plays, owns or employs it. A standard schedule applies only
  while its player plans the anthro as a player.
[[only: admin]]
- Admins schedule any other anthro from the admin panel ("Schedule (admin)").
[[/only]]
- An **Invite** to a closed breeding group shows as unavailable, with a link to open the group to invites, instead of
  not showing at all.

### Inviting into a breeding group
- An anthro's page has an **Invite** button for the breeding groups you own and are in (ones taking invites, or any
  for an anthro you control, which joins at once).

### Train first, then work
- **An anthro can only work at an occupation once it has trained at the skill** (a day of training, or working at it
  before). A day planned working at one it hasn't learned is spent resting, with no pay.
- On an anthro's schedule, the occupations it hasn't learned are listed apart ("train at Cooking first"), so a routine
  can still train first and work later in the week.

### Fiefs and taxes
- **Grant land as a fief** (Court → Fiefs): a lot, or acres of one, to someone of your rank or lower, who swears
  fealty to you for it. It still counts toward your rank, and a vassal holding a fief can't rise above its lord.
  Fiefs can be granted onward; each goes back up the chain in turn.
- **Taxes:** a vassal owes a share of what its household produces (coins earned, and food and lumber at 2 coins
  each until the goods market sets prices), collected each Monday after everyone eats: coins first, then food beyond
  a week's, then lumber. What can't be paid is owed. **Pay your lord** to clear it, or pay ahead: credit covers later
  taxes. The daily report shows what's owed.
- **The tax rate** changes only when the lord offers a new one and the vassal accepts, up or down.
- **Only for cause:** two weeks behind on tax lets the lord seize the fiefs, and the vassal leaves its service. A fief
  also goes back if the vassal swears elsewhere or loses their freedom, or dies without a free child (the eldest
  inherits it, sworn to the lord on the same terms). Fiefs can't be sold. A lord's heir in title keeps the lordship;
  with none, vassals keep their fiefs outright.
- The Land page marks lots that are fiefs.

### Becoming an anthro
- If you don't play an anthro yet, an anthro's page has a **Become** button when nobody plays it (and it isn't a
  noble, up for auction, or dead).

### Granted titles and resets
- **A granted title is a floor.** A noble granted a title by the crown who rises higher by land falls back no
  further than the granted title when the land goes. The crown can revoke the grant: the land's title stays, with
  no floor under it.
- **A reset always leaves at least one barony.** If no court is seated, there's an empty one, held by no one: three
  villages, a town and an expanse nobody manages, and 10,000 to 15,000 acres of the land office's land.
[[only: admin]]
- **Admins choose the new game's court** when resetting: how many of each rank, from none at all (an empty game) to a
  full court, whether the monarch has a consort, and whether they get baronies, settlements and commoners.
[[/only]]

### Marriage
- **Households:** a household has one head and any number of spouses, who share the head's rank as consorts: a
  lion's lionesses are all Queens, equal to each other. Propose on the Court page, to marry someone into your
  household or to marry into theirs. An anthro nobody plays answers at once, and one that says no can't be asked
  again for a week.
- **The household's land counts** toward the head's rank, as does the land of everyone sworn to them.
- **A title earned by land is kept only while the land holds.** Anyone can leave a household, or be sent away,
  without the other's consent, but it costs them both: she's no longer Queen, and without her land he may no
  longer be King. Vassals leaving can bring their lord down the same way. Titles granted by the crown are kept
  regardless.
- **One monarch:** the crown is held by one King or Queen by right; their spouses share it as consorts. A marriage
  ends when either spouse dies or loses their freedom.

### Rising by land
- **Land earns rank.** Any free anthro can take up a rank on the Court page once it holds the land for it:
  **Baronet 500 acres**, Baron 1,500, Viscount 4,500, Earl 13,500, Marquess 40,500 and Duke 121,500 (each three
  times the one below). Any number can hold each rank, and land enough for several ranks earns the highest.
- This replaces taking up the rank above because no one held it.
- **Land sworn to you counts.** The land of everyone sworn to you, and everyone sworn to them, counts toward your
  rank. A commoner with 350 acres and friends holding 150 between them becomes a Baronet, a third of the way to Baron.
- **Swear fealty to a peer, at any rank:** anyone can swear to someone of their own rank or higher. Commoners pick a
  fellow commoner by name on the Court page. No one can swear to someone sworn to them. When a lord loses their
  title, those they outranked are released (commoners are sworn to another lord).
- **The crown is earned by land too:** 364,500 acres, three times a Duke's. With no King, three dukes swearing to a
  fourth make him King. There's still one King and one Queen.
- The Court page shows the land the next rank takes, how much you and those sworn to you hold, and how far along you are.

### Assets
- The Overview and Slaves lists show the **schedule** each anthro follows: a standard schedule, or its own routine.

### Standard schedules
- **Assets → Schedules:** name and keep as many weekly routines as you like. Change one, and every anthro following
  it follows the change.
- An anthro **follows** a standard schedule or its own routine: choose on its schedule page, or for several at once
  under Schedule on the Overview or Slaves. Its own routine is kept for when it stops following. Days planned ahead,
  and birthing, still come first.
- Each follower's day is checked when it comes: one can't breed with itself, or in a group it isn't in.
- An anthro that's sold or freed goes back to its own routine. Removing a schedule sends its followers back to theirs.

### Dams go looking
- Each day a fertile dam goes without breeding, her **chance of going looking for a mate** rises. Most rise about 5%
  a day; some are slower, and some can't go three days without. Any breeding, giving birth, or becoming fertile
  starts her wait over.
- When she goes looking, she turns first to a **breeding group** she's in, then to her **owner**, then a **fellow
  slave**, and lastly to anyone at **her rank**. Owners are told what came of it.
- This replaces the breeding groups' daily 1-in-10 roll.

### Preferences
- A **Preferences** page (Game → Preferences), per player. Its first setting is your **era**: the Dark Ages (as
  before) or the **Renaissance**.
- The Renaissance brings the arts: Painting, Sculpture, Poetry, Architecture, Printing and Theatre, with their
  occupations (Painter, Sculptor, Poet, Architect, Printer, Actor and more), and Composer for Music.
- But in the Renaissance **no one is bred before 14 weeks old**: breeding, breeding thyself, flirts and scheduled or
  group breeding refuse (a flirt follows both players' eras).

### Jobs
- Wages are now **1 to 5 coins a day**. A worker's trade (its best skill and level) shows on the job market.
- Whether an employer **may breed** a worker is a term of the job, shown on the job market. Workers who agree
  generally ask a coin more. Set your own terms with your wage on the game home page.
- What a worker makes at work (its pay for a trade, food it forages, land it clears) goes to its employer.

### Food and upkeep
- **Everyone eats:** each anthro eats 1 food a day. A free anthro (employees too) eats from its own store, and an
  owned one (young ones too) from its owner's, the owner eating first. Every anthro starts with a week's food.
- When the food runs out, meals are **bought at the market** for 5 coins each, while the coins last.
- Anthros on the market (the game's, and any up for auction) are fed there: they never go hungry, and don't eat
  their seller's food.
- An anthro that goes without is **hungry** that day: it can only rest or forage, and breeding it gives no litter. Its
  page says so, and Assets → Goods shows how many days of food you have for everyone you feed.
- **Work pays:** a day's work at an occupation earns coins by skill level (Novice 2, Apprentice 4, Journeyman 8,
  Master 15). **Clearing land** also yields 2 lumber a day, and **building** uses 1 lumber a day (no lumber, no
  building that day).
- What an owned anthro makes (coins, food, lumber, land) goes to its owner, and what it needs (food, lumber to build)
  comes from its owner. An employee's work goes to its employer, but it feeds itself.
- **A daily report** comes as a notification: what each anthro you keep did that day, the food (and lumber) gathered
  and eaten, what's left in store, meals bought, and who went hungry. An anthro kept by someone else gets its own report of its day.

### Schedules
- Every anthro has a **Schedule** (a button on its page, and on the game home page for the one you play): a weekly
  routine of **rest**, **breed**, **work** or **train** for each day, and days planned up to 70 days ahead that break it.
  Its player plans it, or whoever owns or employs it.
- Each day the plan is carried out. **Breed** is with a partner you have or employ, or with a random member of one of
  its breeding groups. **Train** is a day's practice at a skill: Novice, then Apprentice after 7 days, Journeyman after
  28 and Master after 84.
- **Skills** have occupations to go with them: Cooking makes a Baker or a Chef, Archery or Falconry a Hunter,
  Administration a Steward, Reeve or Bailiff, and so on to Witchcraft and Prostitution. **Work** at an occupation is
  practice at its skill too.
- **Work** can also be work that takes no skill, for whoever the anthro works for (its employer, else its owner, or
  itself if it's free): **clearing land** (a quarter acre a day, in the wilds or an expanse of a barony, added to their
  land there), **building** (a day's work on a building going up on their land) or **foraging** (1 to 3 food a day for
  their store, shown under Assets → Goods; food isn't eaten or sold yet).
- **Buildings** (a house, barn, mill, smithy and more) are started on your land from a schedule page, and take a
  number of days of work and some of the lot's acres. Assets → Land shows what stands on each lot. Admins decide what
  can be built.
- The day her litter is due, a dam is **birthing**, and does nothing else.
- On **Assets** (Overview or Slaves), tick anthros and use **Schedule** to give them all the same weekly routine, or
  plan the same day for all of them. Each is checked on its own: one that can't (say, breeding with itself) is
  skipped and you're told why.
- The schedule page shows the next two weeks, as a list or a calendar (your choice is remembered), the anthro's
  skills and what it did lately.

### Herms
- A herm can **breed itself**: choose a litter of 1 to 8 and it sires its own. It's on the game home page (under
  *Your anthros*) when you play a herm, and on the page of any herm you own or employ. The usual rules apply (fertile,
  its day of the week), and it stops once the litter is full.

### Land and lots
- A **lot** is now a piece of land of any size, in acres (it used to be a fixed 0.05 acres). Everyone's land is a
  lot, or several. The market sells lots, priced per acre.
- On **Assets → Land**, split acres off a lot into a new lot, or tick lots and merge them into one. Merged lots must
  lie in the same barony (and the same village, town, city or expanse), and land for sale can't be split or merged.

### Baronies
- All land lies in a **barony**, and maybe in one of its villages, towns, cities or expanses. A barony is held and
  managed by a Baron, or directly by a higher lord (the crown holds its own). Through the Baron, every lord above holds
  it too: a Duke holds his baronies through his Marquesses, and on down. Each village, town, city or expanse is
  managed by a Baronet.
- A barony says where land lies, not who owns it: the lots in it can belong to anyone.
- **Court → Lands** shows the realm's baronies under the lords who hold them, directly or through their vassals. Each
  barony has a page with its villages, towns, cities and expanses, how many people live in each, and all its land.
- The real estate market and **Assets → Land** say where each lot lies. If you hold or manage land, Assets → Land
  shows your baronies too.
- A hereditary title's baronies pass to its successor. Otherwise a barony goes up to its holder's liege, and a
  Baronet's village, town, city or expanse goes back to the barony.

### Where anthros live
- **Fealty decides where an anthro lives:** in the village, town or city of the Baronet it's sworn to (directly, or
  through a knight), or in the barony of the lord it's sworn to. Owned anthros live with their owners. An anthro's
  page says where it lives.
- **Villages:** each villager farms a lot of 10 acres, around the village's core, 10 to 50 acres held by its Baronet.
- **Towns** are a single lot of 40 to 50 acres held by the Baronet; **cities** have one such lot for every 300 people.
  Townsfolk and city folk hold no land of their own.
- **A new game starts empty:** no anthros, no land, no baronies. The first player creates their anthro and can also
  start with a slave to breed with: of their species and their very age, of the opposite sex. A herm can choose one
  that sires or one that carries, or none.
- **Taking up a higher rank:** there are no higher lords yet. A noble can take up the rank above theirs while no one
  holds it, from the Court page (Baron to Viscount, and so on up to King or Queen).

### Young anthros
- Cubs are young until they're fertile. A young anthro belongs to its mother's owner: to her, if she's free, or
  otherwise to whoever owns her. It lives with them and shows as a **Child**.
- A free mother farming a village lot farms 10 more acres for each of her young.
- Once fertile, a young anthro grows up and goes **free**, whoever owns it. One raised by its free mother stays sworn
  where she is, and one raised on her village lot takes its 10 acres as its own lot.

### Flirting
- When you flirt, choose how many times to breed (1 to 8). If they accept, you try that many times, and each try
  that takes adds a cub to the litter. They see how many times you asked for before answering.
- Breeding from an anthro's **Breed** page also takes a number of attempts (1 to 8). Both stop once the dam's
  litter is full, and the breeding notification always says whether a litter is coming.

### Litter sizes
- Each dam has her own largest litter, from 1 to 8 cubs; nobody knows it, but her litters stop there. A daughter's is her mother's, give or
  take one; an anthro with no recorded mother has anywhere from 1 to 8. Breeding and flirting stop once her litter
  is full.

### Aging
- Anthros age and die. Each lives about 52 to 80 weeks; some live longer than others, and nobody knows how long
  their own anthro has.
- When an anthro dies, what it had (coins, land and the anthros it owned) goes to its owner, or if it was free, to its
  eldest living free child, or else to its liege. Its job ends, it leaves its breeding groups, and an unborn litter
  is lost. Hereditary titles pass to the eldest free child.
- If the anthro you play dies, you're told, and you can create or become another. New anthros must be born less than
  52 weeks ago.
- The dead stay in their families' trees and histories, marked **Deceased**.
- Dams stop conceiving 47 to 52 weeks after birth (each on her own day, which nobody knows). After that her page says
  she's no longer fertile, and breeding her as a dam gives no litter ("too old to carry"). Siring never stops.

### Menu and tables
- The game's sections are right in the top menu: **Game, Assets, Market, Social, Court, Wallet, Changes**. On a
  phone, open them from the ☰ button. **Log out** is on every page, and the 🔔 bell stays in the top right.
  - **Assets:** Overview, Slaves, Workers, Land and Goods (coming soon).
  - **Market:** Anthros, Jobs, Goods (coming soon) and Real estate.
  - **Social:** Flirt, Breeding groups and Notifications.
  - **Court:** Nobility, Structure and Lands.
- Long tables are shown a page at a time: choose 10, 25, 50, 100 or 250 rows per page under the table, and your
  choice is remembered. Searching or sorting goes back to the first page, and "select all" selects the rows on the
  page you see.

### Assets
- Anthros own anthros; players only play them. What you have is whatever the anthro you play owns. A free anthro
  owns itself.
- **Assets → Slaves** lists the anthros you own, **Workers** the free anthros you've hired, and **Land** your
  lots.
- **Give** an anthro to another anthro from its page (this used to be "Transfer to a player").
- If a free anthro loses its freedom, the anthros and land it owns go to its new owner.
- An anthro stays in the game if its player leaves.
- Any player can look at any anthro's page, but only its owner can act on it.

### Social
- **Social → Flirt:** flirt with any other anthro as the anthro you play, with an optional line; there's also a
  **Flirt** button on every anthro's page. If they accept, you try breeding there and then. The usual rules apply,
  so it may not lead to a litter. Anthros nobody plays answer for themselves.
- **Social → Breeding groups:** a group belongs to the anthro that formed it, and its owner decides who can join:
  **open**, **by request**, **invite only**, **closed** (the default) or **private** (not listed at all).
  - A group can have just one member; it ends when its last member leaves.
  - Use **Find a group** to join a group or ask to join one, and answer invitations at the top of the page.
  - An anthro owned by someone else can't leave a group on its own: use **Ask to leave**, and its owner decides.
- **Social → Notifications:** repeated notifications are grouped while unread. Eight breedings with the same partner
  show as one notification with an **8×** badge and the latest outcome.

### Court
- **Court → Structure** shows who is sworn to whom, from the King and Queen down to the knights, with how many
  commoners are sworn to each noble and to each branch.
- Every commoner is sworn to a noble: new commoners are sworn to one of the lowest nobles (usually a knight), spread
  evenly, and you're told who. You can switch to another noble on the Court page, but not renounce your liege.
- Owned anthros are sworn to their owners.
- On the Court page, those sworn to you are listed as **Vassals** (nobles), **Followers** (free commoners) and
  **Slaves** (anthros you own). Names link to each anthro's page.
- Nobles are the peers, from Baron up to the crown. Baronets and knights hold titles but aren't nobles, so players
  can become them (and commoners), but not nobles.
- Noble titles and baronetcies are hereditary: if the holder loses its freedom, the title goes to its eldest free
  child, along with its liege and followers. A knighthood is simply lost.

### Breeding
- Both anthros are told every time they're bred: who with, and whether a litter is coming or why not.
- Each dam can only conceive on one day of the week, and nobody knows which. On any other day, breeding her just
  doesn't take. Sires can breed any day once they're fertile.
- Breeding that breaks the rules (not fertile, the wrong roles, a dam already expecting from another day) is still
  recorded in both anthros' histories, with no litter.

### A new game
- A new game starts with a full court (a King, a Queen and their nobles down to the knights) and four commoners for
  every noble, each sworn to them.
- Every anthro starts with 0-100 coins.

## Earlier

- **Court and ranks:** ranks run from King and Queen down to commoner and slave. Free anthros can swear fealty to a
  liege, who receives their land if it's forfeit to the crown.
- **Jobs:** hire free anthros for a daily wage on the market's **Jobs** tab, or offer your own anthro for work from
  your home page. Employers can breed their employees.
- **Real estate:** buy and sell land by the lot on the market's **Real estate** tab.
- **Freedom:** owners can set a debt that grows each day. An anthro that can pay it buys its freedom from its home
  page.
- **Auctions:** sell anthros at auction, bid, or buy now, one at a time or several at once.
- **Breeding groups:** anthros who are, in effect, married, and now and then breed on their own.
- **Notifications and messages:** send messages between anthros. Nobody can tell who plays an anthro.
