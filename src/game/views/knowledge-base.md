How Frontier works: each of the game's systems, and how they fit together. For what's new, see **Docs → Changes**.

## The basics

### You play an anthro
- **Players never own anything themselves.** You play one anthro, and whatever it owns is what you have: its coins,
  its goods, its land and the anthros it owns.
- **Ownership:** an anthro is either **free** (it owns itself), **owned** by another anthro (a slave), or the
  **game's own stock** (listed at auction). The dead are neither.
- **Nobody can see who plays an anthro.** Lists of anthros include ones nobody plays, so they don't give it away.
  Anthros nobody plays answer for themselves (flirts, proposals, fief offers) and live by default days.
- **Any player can look at any anthro's page, but only whoever controls it can act on it.**
- If your account goes, your anthro stays in the game, unplayed.

### Starting out
- **Create an anthro:** a name (1–64 characters; the random-name button follows the gender you pick), a gender, a
  species and, optionally, a birthdate less than 52 weeks ago. With no birthdate it arrives just grown and fertile
  from today, and optionally **a skill**: it starts a Journeyman at it, so it can work its trades at once. Afterwards
  only its name can change.
- **Or become one:** any anthro nobody plays that isn't up for auction, dead, or a noble (Baron and up, or married to
  one). It keeps what it has, and its owner if it has one: you can become a slave. A **Become** button is on the
  anthro's page, and the game home lists them.
- **The choice is permanent.** To play another anthro, **request a reset** from the game home (with a reason); an
  admin decides. The anthro you leave keeps everything.
- **A new game has people already:** at least 100 free commoners, each a Journeyman at a trade: first enough
  farmers (with 10 acres each), fishers, hunters and swineherds to feed them all, then every other trade. The list of
  anthros to become shows each one's job.
- **In a game with nobody at all**, the first player can also take a **starter slave** of their species and age, of the
  opposite sex (a herm chooses one that sires or one that carries).
- **Every new anthro** (players', cubs, the game's) starts with **0–100 coins** and **7 food**.

### The game home
- Your anthro's name, gender, species, rank and whether it can sire or carry; buttons for its wallet, food, land,
  schedule and page (and **Breed**, for one you own that can breed).
- If it's owned: **Buy my freedom**, once its owner has set a debt.
- If it's employed: its employer, wage and **Quit**. If it's free and not employed: the **job market** form.
- A herm you own can **breed itself** here (1–8 tries).

## Time and the day

### The game's calendar
- **The game keeps its own calendar**, apart from ours. A new game starts on the date the admins choose
  (**1 January 1200** unless they pick another).
- **Its time runs at a pace:** so many game days for each real day, from **0.25** (a game day every four real days) to
  **24** (a game day every real hour). Each player can ask for a pace under **Game → Preferences**; the game runs at
  the **slowest pace anyone playing asks for**, or a game day each real day if nobody asks. A change of pace counts
  from then on.
- **The clock** runs at the right of each section's bar (on a wide screen), with the paces under it: the one the
  game runs at is solid and pulsing, the one you ask for solid, the rest outlined. Click one to ask for it, and yours
  again to stop asking. The Changes page also shows how long until the next game day in real time.

### The daily turnover
- **Each game day's business is done in turn,** for everyone at once, when the game is next visited: however many game
  days have passed, every one of them happens, in order (up to a year's worth at a visit).
- **Plans made during a game day take effect the next,** once that day's schedules have run.
- **Auctions keep real time:** a 3-day auction lasts three real days, whatever the game's pace.
- **Maintenance:** while the game is down, its clock stands still; no days go by.

### The order of the day
1. **Births:** litters due are born.
2. **Coming of age:** young anthros whose fertile day has come grow up.
3. **Deaths:** anthros whose time has come die.
4. **Meals:** everyone eats (or goes hungry).
5. **Schedules:** each anthro's planned day is carried out.
6. **Default days:** anthros nobody plans live by their defaults.
7. **Taxes:** vassals are assessed (and on Mondays, taxes are collected).
8. **The daily report** goes out.
9. **Auctions** past their end are settled.
10. **Wages** are paid.
11. **Dams' urges:** some dams go looking for a mate.
12. **Fiefs, titles, lieges and holdings** are brought into line.

Because meals come before work, an anthro that goes hungry is hungry for that whole day's work.

### The week
- Weekly routines, each dam's conceiving day and the **Monday tax day** follow the game's own weekdays (1 January
  1200 was a Saturday).
- Ages are in **weeks**.

## Anthros

### Species and genders
- **Species** are listed by family (canines, felines, bears, birds and fish, and more). A cub is one parent's
  species or the other's, evenly.
- **Genders** decide who can **sire** and who can **carry**, and how an anthro **presents** (its symbol and the names it
  gets). The usual ones: Male (sires), Female (carries), Herm (both), Trans male (carries, presents male) and Trans
  female (sires, presents female). A newborn's gender is drawn by each gender's birth odds.
- A gender that can do neither can't breed.

### Growing up and growing old
| Stage | When |
|---|---|
| Newborn | Under **5 weeks**: it only rests, and its days can't be planned. |
| Young ("Child") | Until it's fertile, **9–12 weeks** (63–84 days) after birth. |
| Grown | From its fertile day. |
| Past carrying | A dam stops conceiving **47–52 weeks** after birth. Siring never stops. |
| Death | A lifespan of **52–80 weeks**; a dam lives at least 20 weeks past her last fertile day. |

- Nobody knows their own anthro's lifespan or exact fertile days. An anthro dies of old age; hunger doesn't kill.
- **Coming of age:** a young anthro belongs to its mother's owner until it's fertile. Then it goes **free**, whoever
  owned it (any debt is cleared). One raised by a free commoner mother is sworn where she is; one raised on her
  village lot takes 10 acres of it as its own lot.

### Death and inheritance
- **The heir** is its owner (if it was owned), else its **eldest living free child**, else its **liege**.
- The heir gets its **coins**, its **goods**, its **land** and the **anthros it owned**. With no heir, its coins and
  goods leave the game, its land goes to the land office and its anthros go free.
- **If it was up for auction,** the auction ends and the highest bid goes back to the bidder.
- A **hereditary title** passes to its eldest free child who doesn't already rank as high (see *Ranks and titles*).
- Its job ends (and its employees' jobs), its marriages end, it leaves its breeding groups, an unborn litter is lost
  and pending flirts are withdrawn.
- **If the anthro you play dies,** you're told, and you can create or become another.
- The dead stay in family trees and histories, marked **Deceased**.

### Names
- Random names follow how an anthro presents (male, female, or neutral names), avoiding names already used in the
  same household where it can.

## Breeding

### Who can breed whom
- **The owner** decides for an anthro it owns; a free anthro's player decides for it.
- **Employees can't be bred** by their employer: they're hired to work. A free employee's cubs are her own.
- **A played slave can't breed itself** as it likes: its owner decides.

### Ways to breed
- **An anthro's Breed page:** choose a partner and 1–8 tries.
- **Assets, with selected:** each selected sire breeds a random selected dam, for 1–8 rounds.
- **Breed thyself:** a herm sires its own litter, 1–8 tries.
- **Schedules:** a day of breeding with a partner, or with a random member of a breeding group.
- **Flirting** and **dams' urges** (below).

### Conceiving
- **Each dam conceives on only one day of the week,** and nobody knows which. On any other day, breeding her just
  doesn't take. Sires can breed any day.
- **Pregnancy lasts 63 days** from the first breeding that takes. Every further breeding that takes **on that same
  day** (with any sire) adds a cub; on later days she's already expecting.
- **Litter size:** each dam has her own largest litter, **1–8 cubs**, which nobody knows. A daughter's is her
  mother's, give or take one. Breeding stops once her litter is full.
- **No litter comes** if either is dead, hungry, not yet fertile, too old to carry, up for auction, or can't fill
  the role; the breeding is still recorded, with the reason.
- **Both parents are told every time:** who with, and whether a litter is coming or why not. Repeats with the same
  partner are grouped (a **×8** badge, say).

### Birth
- On the due date the dam is **birthing**, and does nothing else.
- **The cubs belong to the dam's owner** (herself, if she's free), and live with them.
- Each cub has its own gender (by birth odds), one parent's species, and its own hidden traits. Skills aren't
  inherited: cubs learn a trade from a parent as they grow (see *Default days*).
- A free mother farming a village lot farms **10 more acres** for each cub.

### Dams' urges
- Each day a fertile dam goes without breeding, her **chance of going looking for a mate** rises: most by about 5%
  a day; some slower (1–2%), some much faster (some can't go three days without).
- Any breeding, a birth, or becoming fertile starts her wait over.
- When she goes looking she turns first to a **breeding group** she's in, then her **owner**, then a **fellow slave**,
  then anyone at **her rank**. Her owner is told what came of it.
- Only owned or free, grown, fed dams that aren't expecting or up for auction go looking; the game's stock never does.
- Her conceiving day still applies, so many of these don't take.

### The Renaissance
- In the **Renaissance** era (see *Preferences*), no one under **14 weeks** old is bred.

## Social

### Flirting
- **Social → Flirt,** or **Flirt** on any anthro's page: flirt as the anthro you play, with an optional line (up to
  200 characters), asking to breed **1–8 times**.
- A played anthro accepts or declines; one **nobody plays answers at once, yes half the time**.
- If they accept, you try breeding there and then, as many times as asked (stopping when her litter is full). The
  usual rules apply, so it may not lead to a litter. Cubs belong to the dam's owner.
- One flirt at a time between two anthros; none with anthros up for auction.

### Breeding groups
- **Social → Breeding groups.** A group belongs to the anthro that formed it (which needn't be a member), and its
  owner decides who joins: **open** (join at once), **by request**, **invite only**, **closed** (the default) or
  **private** (not listed).
- A group can have just one member, and ends when its last member leaves. When its owner dies, the longest-standing
  member takes it over.
- Add anthros you control at once; invite others (not into a closed group). An anthro's page has an **Invite**
  button for groups you own and are in.
- An owned anthro can't leave on its own: **Ask to leave**, and its owner (or employer) decides.
- **What groups do:** a dam's urge turns to her groups first, and a schedule can breed with a random member of one.

### Notifications and messages
- **Social → Notifications** (the 🔔 bell): notifications belong to the anthro, so whoever plays it sees them.
  Repeats are grouped while unread. **Deleting** one takes it off your list, but administrators can still review it.
- **Messages** go from anthro to anthro (up to 2,000 characters) and show only the sending anthro's name.

### Preferences
- **Game → Preferences:** your **era**. The **Dark Ages** (the default), or the **Renaissance**, which brings the arts
  (Painting, Sculpture, Poetry, Architecture, Printing and Theatre, with their occupations, and Composer for Music)
  but no breeding under 14 weeks. The era of whoever acts applies; a flirt follows both players' eras.

## Schedules

### What an anthro does each day
- Every anthro has a **Schedule** (a button on its page): a **weekly routine** of **rest**, **breed**, **work** or
  **train** for each day, and **days planned** up to **70 days** ahead that break it.
- **Work** is an occupation, or work that takes no skill: **clearing land**, **building** or **foraging**.
- An anthro's day comes from, in order:
  1. **Birthing**, if her litter is due;
  2. **a day planned** ahead;
  3. **a standard schedule** it follows;
  4. **its own weekly routine** (days left out are rest);
  5. **its default day** (below).
- The schedule page shows the next two weeks (as a list or a calendar), its skills, and what it did lately.

### Who plans
- **Its player, its owner, or its employer.** Anthros under 5 weeks only rest, and can't be planned.

### Standard schedules
- **Assets → Schedules:** name and keep weekly routines. Change one, and everyone following it follows the change.
- An anthro follows a standard schedule or its own routine (its own is kept for when it stops). It follows only
  while the schedule's keeper can still plan it: sold or freed, it goes back to its own.
- Each follower's day is checked when it comes: one can't breed with itself, or in a group it isn't in.
- **Several at once:** on Assets (Overview or Slaves), tick anthros and use **Schedule** to set a routine, a standard
  schedule or one day for all. Each is checked on its own; any skipped are listed.

### Default days
For anthros with no routine of their own:
- **Under 5 weeks:** rest.
- **Young:** learn a trade from a parent (a cub that presents as male from its father, others from their mother;
  the other parent if that one can't teach, else a random trade), training until it's a **Journeyman** (28 days),
  then working it.
- **A grown slave** works its trade (once it's a Journeyman) for its owner, or rests.
- **An anthro that keeps itself** (free, nobody plays it, not employed, no routine) **lives by a trade:** a trade it
  works at as at least a Journeyman, every day (never one that needs land). It **buys the materials** its trade needs
  as far as its coins go, **sells what it makes** (keeping any food), forages instead when it's hungry or out of food
  and coins, and **buys food** at the market toward a week's worth.
- An **employed** anthro with no routine rests (unless it was hired to work the occupation it offered).
- A routine saved as all rest counts as no routine.

### Carrying out a day
- **Hungry:** a hungry anthro can only rest or forage (or give birth); anything else becomes rest.
- **Train:** a day's practice at a skill.
- **Work at an occupation:** needs the skill learned (at least a day's practice); otherwise it rests, unpaid. It
  makes its recipe's goods for the worker's **master** from the master's materials, or pays the master in coins (a
  service job), by level (see *Work and goods*), and is practice too.
- **Clear land:** a **quarter acre** and **2 lumber** a day, in the wilds or an expanse, for its master.
- **Build:** a day's work on a building, using **1 lumber**.
- **Forage:** **1–3 food** for its master's store.
- **Breed:** with the partner or group planned; whoever planned it must still control both.
- **Its master** is its **employer**, else its **owner** (a free anthro is its own). What it makes goes to its
  master; the game's own anthros make nothing for anyone.

## Work and goods

### Three kinds of work
- **Producers** make goods from nothing but their labour (and a farmer, land): crops, wool, meat, fish, ore.
- **Craftsmen** turn goods into goods: a brewer makes ale from grain and hops.
- **Service jobs** need no materials and make no goods: they're **paid in coins**, by level.
- **The goods are a producer's or craftsman's pay:** they earn no coins, but keep what they make, or sell it at the
  market.

### A day's work
- A producer's or craftsman's work follows one of its **recipes**: when planning, choose which ("Farmer: Hops"),
  or it works the first. A day makes **1, 2, 3 or 5 units** by level: a Journeyman brewer makes 3 ale from 3 grain
  and 3 hops.
- **Materials come from its master's store,** and what it makes goes there (its master: its employer, else its
  owner, or itself if free). **Short of materials,** it makes as many units as they allow; with none, it rests (and
  learns nothing).
- **A farmer needs land:** its master must hold at least **10 acres** (a villager's lot counts), or it rests.
- **What's made counts toward taxes,** like any work (see *Taxes*).

## Food and goods

### Goods and stores
- Each anthro has a **store** of goods: food, lumber, and whatever work makes (see *Work and goods*).
- **Food** is any good marked food: food itself (from foraging or the market), vegetables, meat, fish, bread and hot
  meals. **Lumber** comes from clearing land, and goes into buildings and crafts.
- **Assets → Goods** shows your store, how many you feed, food eaten a day, days of food left, and the meal price.

### Everyone eats
- Each anthro eats **1 food a day** (any food good, the cheapest first), from its **keeper's** store: its **owner**
  (a free anthro keeps itself). The keeper eats first. **Employees feed themselves.** Young anthros eat from their mother's owner.
- **When the food runs out,** the keeper **buys meals** at the market's food price, while its coins last.
- **Anyone left unfed is hungry** that day: it can only rest or forage, breeding it gives no litter, and its page
  says so.
- **Anthros on the market** (the game's, and any up for auction) are fed there: never hungry, and they don't eat
  their seller's food.
- Food in an owned anthro's own store isn't what it eats: it eats from its owner's.

### The goods market
- **Market → Goods:** buy from the market and sell to it (1–10,000 at a time), at its prices, through your played
  anthro's wallet and store.
- **Admins set the prices:** a buy price (what you pay) and a sell price (what it pays you), or neither. A good never
  sells for more than it costs. To start, food and lumber cost **5** and sell for **2**.
- **Donate:** an anthro's page has a **Donate** button, to give it coins (into its own wallet) or goods (into its
  keeper's store).

## Money

### Wallets
- **Every anthro has a wallet** of whole coins; you spend from your played anthro's. A wallet can't go below zero.
- **Finances → Wallet:** your balance, coins held in your winning bids, and the last 100 entries.
- **Where coins come from:** service work (paid by the game), selling to the market, wages, sales, gifts, inheritance and
  starting balances. **Where they go:** meals and market purchases, wages, bids, land, taxes and gifts. Coins paid
  to the game (meals, market purchases, the game's auctions, the land office) leave the game.
- **An owned anthro still has its own wallet:** its earnings go to its owner, but gifts to it are its own, and that's
  what it buys its freedom with.

### The finances report
- **Finances → Report:** what your anthro can expect to earn and spend over the **next 7 days**, from what's planned
  now: your household's work (the goods it makes and the materials it uses, or its pay, at today's levels; foraging
  counted at 2 food a day), wages paid and earned, food and
  meals to buy, and taxes to your lord and from your vassals, with your balance before and after, and the next tax
  day.

## Jobs

### Looking for work
- On the game home, a **free anthro that isn't employed** can ask a **wage of 1–15 coins a day** (or none: not
  looking), and offer **an occupation** it has learned (shown with its level and the wage to expect).
- **The wage to expect** is what its work pays at its level (1 coin with no skill).
- Anthros nobody plays (free, untitled) ask by the same rule, from their best skill.

### Hiring
- **Market → Jobs** lists anthros looking for work: trade, species, age and wage. **Hire**
  one, or several at once. You can't hire yourself, or anyone employed or up for auction.
- **Wages are paid a day in advance,** from the employer's wallet, for each day due. If the employer can't pay, the job
  ends and both are told.
- An employee who offered an occupation (and has no routine of its own) starts working it every day.
- **Employees** work for their employer (everything they make goes to it), are planned by it, and feed themselves.
  **They're hired to work:** their employer can't breed them, or plan them to breed.
- **A job ends** when the employer lets the employee go (wages paid aren't returned), the employee quits, loses its
  freedom, or either dies. It's then back on the job market at its asking wage.
- **Assets → Workers** lists your employees and what you pay them each day.

## Auctions and freedom

### Selling at auction
- **The owner** puts an anthro up for auction from its page or from Assets (several at once): a **starting bid**
  (at least 1), an optional **buy-now price**, and **1, 3 or 7 days**. A free anthro can sell itself. No fees.
- **While listed,** it can't be bred, given away, hired, flirt, marry or buy its freedom, and it's fed by the market.
- **The seller** (or an admin) can cancel only while there are no bids, or **close now** (sold to the highest
  bidder, if any).

### Bidding
- **Market → Anthros** lists open auctions, soonest-ending first.
- The first bid is at least the starting bid; each after is **5% more** (at least 1 coin more). **Your bid is taken
  from your wallet at once,** and returned if you're outbid (you're told).
- A bid at the buy-now price buys it at once. You can't bid on your own auction. Bidders show only as "Bidder N".
- You can bid on several at once from the list.

### When an auction ends
- **Sold:** the highest bidder's anthro owns it, the seller is paid, and any debt is cleared. **A free anthro sold loses
  its freedom:** its title, job and marriages, and its land and anthros go to the buyer.
- **Unsold:** it stays with the seller. One of the game's own that doesn't sell goes free.

### Giving an anthro away
- **Give** from its page: to any other living anthro. A free anthro can give itself away, losing its freedom
  as if sold.

### Buying freedom
- **The owner sets a debt** on an anthro it owns (at least 1 coin), and how much it grows each day.
- **The anthro pays it from its own wallet** (from the game home) and goes free. Not while it's up for auction.
- A change of owner, or coming of age, clears the debt.

## Land

### Lots
- Land comes in **lots**, of any size in acres (to the hundredth). Every lot lies in a **barony** (and maybe in one of
  its villages, towns, cities or expanses), or in **the wilds**.
- **Only a free anthro holds land,** and only a free anthro you play can buy, sell, split or merge it.
- **Assets → Land:** your lots by barony, with their buildings, and your holdings.
  - **Split** acres off a lot into a new lot (not the acres a building stands on, nor a lot for sale).
  - **Merge** lots with the same tenure, in the same barony and part, none for sale. The oldest keeps its number.

### Real estate
- **Market → Real estate:** lots for sale at a **fixed price**, cheapest per acre first. Buy one outright.
- **Sell** a lot, or part of one (split off as it sells; only acres free of buildings), for at least 1 coin. One
  listing per lot; take it off whenever you like. **Fiefs can't be sold.**
- The land office sells new land (coins paid to it leave the game).

### Clearing land
- A day's **clearing** adds a **quarter acre** (and 2 lumber) to its master's own land in the wilds or an expanse:
  to its largest lot there (never a fief), or a new one. It's new land, and there's no limit.

### Losing land
- **Losing freedom** (sold or given away): fiefs go back to their lords, and the rest goes to the new owner.
- **Death:** to the heir (see *Death and inheritance*).

## Buildings
- **Start a building** on your land from an anthro's schedule page. It costs nothing to start, but takes a number of
  **days of work** and some of the lot's **acres**:

| Building | Days | Acres |
|---|---|---|
| House | 10 | 0.25 |
| Barn | 15 | 0.5 |
| Granary | 15 | 0.5 |
| Workshop | 12 | 0.25 |
| Smithy | 15 | 0.25 |
| Bakery | 12 | 0.25 |
| Mill | 30 | 0.5 |
| Inn | 30 | 0.5 |
| Chapel | 40 | 1 |

- **Each day of building uses 1 lumber** from the lot holder's store (no lumber, no progress). It needs no skill.
- Assets → Land shows what stands on each lot, and how far along. The acres a building stands on can't be split off,
  sold apart or granted as a fief. Admins decide what can be built.
- Buildings stand on land; so far they don't produce anything.

## Baronies and where anthros live

### Baronies
- **All land lies in a barony,** held by a **Baron** or higher. Through the Baron, every lord above holds it too.
- A barony's **parts** are **villages**, **manors** (a village around a knight's estate), **towns**, **cities** and
  **expanses**. **Knights** manage villages, manors and expanses; **Baronets and higher** manage towns and cities. A
  part with no manager is managed by the barony's holder.
- **A barony says where land lies, not who owns it.**
- **Court → Lands** shows the realm's baronies under the lords who hold them; each barony's page shows its parts
  (manager, people, acres) and all its lots.
- The crown grants baronies. A holder below Baron passes the barony to its liege (if a Baron or higher); a manager
  below its part's rank lets it go.
- **Succession:** a hereditary title's baronies and parts pass with it; otherwise a barony goes up to its holder's
  liege, and a part back to the barony.

### Where anthros live
- **Fealty decides where an anthro lives:** in the part managed by, or the barony held by, the nearest lord up its
  chain of fealty (itself included). **Owned anthros live with their owners.** An anthro's page says where it lives.
- **Villages and manors:** each villager farms **10 acres** around the village's core (10–50 acres, the manager's).
- **Towns** are one lot of 40–50 acres; **cities** have one for every 300 people. Townsfolk hold no land.

## Ranks and titles

### The ranks
| Rank | Noble | Hereditary | Land to earn it |
|---|---|---|---|
| King / Queen | yes | yes | 364,500 acres |
| Duke / Duchess | yes | yes | 121,500 |
| Marquess / Marchioness | yes | yes | 40,500 |
| Earl / Countess | yes | yes | 13,500 |
| Viscount / Viscountess | yes | yes | 4,500 |
| Baron / Baroness | yes | yes | 1,500 |
| Baronet / Baronetess | no | yes | 500 |
| Knight / Dame | no | no | granted only |
| Commoner | no | no | |
| Slave | no | no | |

- **A slave's rank is always Slave.** Slaves can't hold a title or swear fealty.
- **A consort takes their spouse's rank** (see *Marriage*).

### Rising by land
- **Land earns rank.** Any free anthro takes up the rank its land earns from the **Court** page (**Become** …),
  straight to the highest it earns. Each rank takes **three times the land** of the one below. Any number can hold
  each rank; the crown is held by one monarch by right.
- **The land counted** is your own, your **household's** (your spouses'), and that of **everyone sworn to you**, and
  everyone sworn to them, down the chain. Fiefs you granted count too.
- **A vassal holding a fief can't rise above its lord.**
- The Court page shows the land the next rank takes, how much you and those sworn to you hold, and how far along you
  are.

### Keeping a title
- **A title earned by land is kept only while the land holds.** Lose the land (or vassals, or spouses) and it falls
  to the rank the land still earns; you're told.
- **A granted title is a floor.** The crown can grant titles (Knight to Duke); a title granted doesn't depend on land,
  and one who rises higher by land falls back no further than the grant. The crown can revoke a grant: if you've
  risen above it, only the floor goes.

### Inheriting titles
- **Hereditary titles** (Baronet and up) pass on death or loss of freedom to the **eldest free child** who doesn't
  already rank as high, with the holder's liege, those sworn to it, and its baronies. The crown passes only if held
  by right.
- **A knighthood** is simply lost.

## Fealty, fiefs and taxes

### Swearing fealty
- **Swear fealty** on the Court page to anyone of **your rank or higher** (commoners pick a fellow commoner by name).
  No one can swear to someone sworn to them.
- **Commoners are always sworn to someone:** a new or unsworn commoner is sworn to a holder of the lowest title held
  who has the fewest commoners, and told. A commoner can switch lords but not renounce; titled anthros can hold no
  liege.
- **Owned anthros are sworn to their owners.** When a lord loses rank, those who now outrank it are released.
- On the Court page, those sworn to you are listed as **Vassals** (titled), **Followers** (free commoners) and
  **Slaves**. **Court → Structure** shows who is sworn to whom.

### Fiefs
- **Court → Fiefs:** grant a lot, or acres of one (free of buildings), **as a fief** to someone free and grown, of
  your rank or lower, at a **tax rate of 0–50%**. They swear fealty to you for it. One nobody plays accepts at 20% or
  less.
- **A fief still counts toward your rank** (your vassal is sworn to you). It **can't be sold**, but can be granted
  onward; each goes back up the chain in turn.
- **A fief goes back to its lord** if the vassal swears elsewhere, loses its freedom, or dies without a free child
  who may swear to the lord (that child inherits it, sworn on the same terms, with its balance). If the lord is gone
  without an heir in title, the vassal keeps it outright.
- **The tax rate** changes only when the lord offers a new one and the vassal accepts (one nobody plays accepts up to
  20%, or its current rate if higher).

### Taxes
- **A vassal owes its rate of what its household produces each day:** coins earned at service work, and the goods
  made or gathered, valued at what the market pays for them. Wages, sales and gifts aren't taxed.
- **Collected every Monday,** after everyone has eaten and worked: **coins** first, then **food** beyond a week's
  worth for the household, then its **other goods**. What can't be paid is owed.
- **Pay your lord** any time to clear what's owed, or to pay ahead: credit covers later taxes.
- **Two weeks behind** lets the lord **seize** the fiefs: they go back, the arrears are written off, and the vassal
  leaves the lord's service.
- The daily report shows what's owed.

## Marriage
- **A household** has one **head** and any number of **spouses**. Everyone in it must be free and grown; a spouse is
  in one household, and a head can't also be a spouse.
- **Propose** on the Court page, to marry someone into your household or to marry into theirs. One nobody plays
  answers at once, **yes half the time**. After a no, you can't ask them again for **a week**.
- **Spouses share the head's rank as consorts:** a lion's lionesses are all Queens, equal to each other. There's one
  monarch by right; the monarch's spouses are consorts.
- **The household's land counts toward the head's rank.**
- **Anyone can leave, or be sent away,** without the other's consent, but it costs both: the spouse loses the rank,
  and without the spouse's land the head may lose a land-earned title.
- **A marriage ends** when either spouse dies or loses their freedom.

## The daily report
- Each day, **everyone who keeps anthros gets a report** as a notification: what each anthro did, food (and lumber)
  gathered, eaten and left, meals bought, tax owed, and who went hungry.
- **An anthro kept by someone else** gets its own report of its day.
